ResQ Nexus fuses satellite monitoring, citizen ground reports, and a priority-based allocation engine into one system, so a flood, cyclone, or earthquake response is routed by urgency and proximity, not by whoever calls first.

How an incident moves through the system

Three stages, each doing one job well — data comes in from more than one source, gets weighed for trust before anything is dispatched, and the outcome is a ranked, explainable dispatch list rather than a black box.

Sense

Multi-source intake

Satellite-derived flood and fire extent, citizen ground reports, and official feeds all land in the same intake pipeline, each tagged with its source and an initial confidence score.

Decide

Verification & allocation

Reports within the same area and time window are merged; once a report clears a confidence threshold, the allocation engine ranks it by severity and matches the nearest available resource.

Act

Dispatch & accountability

Alerts go out to the right role, not everyone at once, and are tracked through acknowledgment, with automatic escalation if nobody responds in time.

Data sources

Wide but slow satellite coverage, combined with narrow but fast ground truth from citizens — neither is reliable enough alone.

Satellite / remote sensingFlood extent and fire hotspots from sources such as ISRO Bhuvan Disaster Services and Sentinel imagery.
Citizen reportsDirect incident submissions with location, photos, and description from people on the ground.
Official / sensor feedsMeteorological and seismic data streams, treated as high-confidence sources.
